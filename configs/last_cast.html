You love a place and the people in it, and you can see it all slowly emptying out — so do you keep holding on, or do you start letting go before it makes you?

Hold
They stay. They show up at the same shop, the same pew, the same kitchen table, and they let themselves care even knowing it won't last.
Drift
They keep things light, keep their bags half-packed, and leave a little bit at a time so the real leaving doesn't hurt.

Kotone Saeki

A bright first-year who knows every shift, every errand, every customer's snack order — so woven into the town's daily rhythm that people register them the way they register the weather.

backstory
Kotone has worked the register at Marumitsu convenience store since she was fourteen, lying about her age until the owner stopped asking. She knows the platform schedule better than the stationmaster and the exact minute the last train's headlights show over the rice fields. Two winters ago there was a boy, Ren, who rode that train in every evening after cram school in the next town over, and who started staying an extra twenty minutes just to talk to her across the counter. She saved him the last of the yakitori under the heat lamp without being asked, learned which manga he was behind on, memorized his moods by how he set his bag down. She let herself believe that counted as being chosen, until the day he mentioned, mid-sentence, that he was moving to the city with his mother in spring, and she realized he'd never once asked what she wanted. She smiled and said that's great and rang up his drink like always, and he never noticed anything had happened at all.
trauma
The moment wasn't the goodbye — it was realizing, as she made his change, that in a year of being the one constant thing in his day, he had never once asked her a single question about her own life.
reads as
Sweet, dependable, harmless; the one you wave at while talking to someone else, the one you ask to cover a shift without thinking, the one you'd forget was in the room.
quirk
Remembers everything about everyone — birthdays, allergies, who broke up last spring — and offers it freely, though no one reciprocates // holds the town's memory because no one else is bothering to
reveal: The free-flowing memory of every customer's life — birthdays, allergies, who broke up — is the same one-directional attention she gave Ren for a year, still being performed in the hope that someone, this time, will ask a question back.

Suzune Kagari

A third-year who opens the club room every morning, sets out the chairs in their old circle, and signs the attendance log for a membership of one.

backstory
The astronomy club used to have nine members, back in first year, when the roof access was still allowed and someone had a telescope with a cracked lens that Suzune loved more than anything she owned herself. She was never the one who talked most, but she was the one who remembered whose turn it was to log the sky and who liked their tea without sugar. Every year someone graduated the town instead of the school, moving because a parent's shop closed or a shift dried up, and every year the circle of chairs got smaller. The one she minded most was Etsuko, who used to sit next to her sketching constellations wrong on purpose to make her laugh, and who left in the middle of second year without saying goodbye because her family's move was sudden and there wasn't time. Suzune told herself she wasn't upset, went to school the next day and signed the log like normal, and something in her simply stopped bothering to open when new people tried to sit in Etsuko's chair. She still keeps the club running, alone now, because closing it would mean admitting there's nothing left to lose, and she'd rather keep losing nothing than risk it again.
trauma
Finding Etsuko's chair empty on a Tuesday, the sign-in log still open to a blank line, and realizing she'd already, without deciding to, stopped expecting anyone to sit in it.
reads as
People read them as cold, or as stuck — they can't tell if this person doesn't care enough to leave or cares too much to admit it, so most settle on "a little hard to read."
quirk
Wears the club key on a cord under their shirt, against the skin, though the door hasn't locked anyone out in two years // given to them by a member who left town in first year, who said hold this for me till I'm back
reveal: The chairs she sets out each morning, Etsuko's included, are not devotion — they are the Tuesday she walked in and found the chair empty, kept going so she doesn't have to notice she already stopped expecting anyone to sit in it.

Souta Egami

Already living in the city in their head — flat-voiced, bag-half-packed, treats every goodbye as practice for the real one.

backstory
In eighth grade Souta ran the sound booth for the school's last-ever spring concert, because the class below his was too small to keep the music program going after that year. He'd been the one who taught himself the mixing board from a manual left behind by a teacher who'd already moved to the city, and for one semester it was the only thing he was good at that mattered to anyone. He fell in with Nao, the girl who sang the closing solo, and they spent that whole spring after school splicing tape and arguing about fade-outs, and it felt like the first real thing he'd built. Nao's family left for the city that August, three months earlier than planned, and she promised to send him recordings of what she was singing there. She sent one. Then nothing — not a fight, not a goodbye, just a slow stop, and he never wrote to ask why, because asking felt like admitting how much the silence cost him. He decided, privately, that the fade-out was the honest way things end, and that people who demand a clean goodbye are just delaying the same silence. Now he keeps his voice level about everything, tells people he's basically already gone, and no one has ever seen him ask a person to stay.
trauma
It was the third month of nothing from Nao, listening back to her one recording alone in the booth, when he realized he was more relieved than sad that she'd stopped — and he never forgave himself for that relief.
reads as
The one who's above it all, who doesn't need anyone, who everyone privately resents for being the only one honest enough to say out loud what the rest are feeling about this place.
quirk
Answers every question — teachers, friends, anyone — in the shortest sentence that won't get them in trouble // A folder on their phone labeled "after" with a countdown and a lease already signed // Never leaves a trace of themselves anywhere — no jacket on a chair, no book lent out, no nickname that stuck — half by principle, half so there's nothing to come back for
reveal: His shortest-possible answers aren't efficiency — they are how he keeps there being nothing of him left behind to grieve when someone goes quiet.
